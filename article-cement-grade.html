Choosing the Right Cement Grade for Your Site

5 MIN READ · FIG. 03
← Back to Articles

Not all cement is interchangeable

"Cement" on a Pakistani site almost always means one of three types: OPC, PPC, or SRC. They're not upgrades of each other — each is suited to a different job, and using the wrong one can cost you either strength, durability, or money you didn't need to spend.

OPC — Ordinary Portland Cement

The default choice for most structural work. OPC comes in grades — commonly 33, 43, and 53 in the subcontinent — where the number refers to the minimum compressive strength (in MPa) at 28 days. Grade 43 is the most common all-rounder for RCC framed structures; Grade 53 is used where higher early strength is needed, such as precast elements or fast-track construction.

PPC — Portland Pozzolana Cement

PPC blends OPC clinker with a pozzolanic material, usually fly ash. It gains strength more slowly than OPC but produces less heat during hydration, which matters in mass concrete pours (foundations, dams, thick rafts) where heat build-up can cause cracking. It also tends to be more durable against water and chemical attack over the long term, at a slightly lower cost than OPC.

SRC — Sulphate Resisting Cement

Used specifically where soil or groundwater has high sulphate content, which attacks ordinary concrete over time. Common in foundations near saline groundwater, coastal sites, and some industrial environments. It's a specialist choice — don't use it as a general-purpose cement, since it offers no particular advantage where sulphate attack isn't a risk.

Site tip If you're unsure whether a site has a sulphate risk, a soil test is far cheaper than discovering the problem 10 years into a building's life. Don't default to SRC "just in case" without data — it adds cost without benefit if the risk isn't real.

How to decide

  • Standard columns, beams, and slabs on a normal site → OPC 43, or OPC 53 if you need faster formwork turnaround.
  • Large foundation pours, rafts, or mass concrete → PPC, to control heat of hydration and reduce cracking risk.
  • Foundations in sulphate-affected soil or near seawater → SRC, confirmed by a soil/water test.

The right grade isn't about which cement is "strongest" on the bag — it's about matching the cement's behaviour to what the specific pour actually needs.
